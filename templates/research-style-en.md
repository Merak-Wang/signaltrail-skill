# English style card: question-led explainer with dry wit

An original composite voice, not an impersonation of a named author. The short source excerpts and links are in the main report, section 9, W04–W06. This card deliberately avoids repeating them.

## Reader goal

Make a difficult mechanism intelligible and worth following. Give the reader a real question, concrete actors, explicit assumptions and an answer proportionate to the evidence. Use connected paragraphs, not a succession of slogans or presentation cards.

## Voice

**Concrete, not grandiose.** Begin with an observable tension or a bounded question. The opening must not invent an anecdote, meeting, quotation or motive.

**Lucid, not overconfident.** Put conditions next to conclusions. Explain a necessary term at the point where the reader needs it. Preserve uncertainty without burying the main answer in ritual disclaimers.

**Dry, not flippant.** A small contrast between a claim and a constraint can carry the humour. Do not add a joke quota. No comic treatment of casualties, disasters or personal victims.

## Narrative order

Hook → why it matters → relevant context → mechanism → distribution of benefits and costs → strongest countercase → next observable signal.

This is a flexible arc, not a required set of headings. Retain the underlying geopolitical, technical and market questions. If a link is not evidenced, leave it unresolved rather than force a grand narrative.

## Original examples — hypothetical, not news

Avoid: “The revolutionary breakthrough changes everything overnight.”
Prefer: “The price changed overnight. The rest of the cost structure did not.”
Reason: A defined contrast can be examined; universal transformation cannot simply be asserted.

Avoid: “The savings will inevitably flow to consumers.”
Prefer: “Whether customers receive the saving depends on who can keep it.”
Reason: The second sentence opens an incentive question rather than assuming its answer.

Avoid: “Only time will tell.”
Prefer: “A comparable invoice and retry rate would tell us more than another launch slide.”
Reason: The reader is left with a testable next step.

## Illustrated reading

Use a figure only when it resolves a specific question in the surrounding text. Label hypothetical calculations as hypothetical. Keep original image captions separate from editorial captions. Use a complete text/table fallback when a relevant asset is unavailable. Do not imply causation by layout alone.

## Semantic invariants

Do not change entities, values, units, dates, baselines, attribution, negation, uncertainty, temporal status or causal direction. The English version may differ from the Chinese version in rhythm and paragraph structure, not in its claim ledger.

## Source techniques

W04 Twain: understated structural contrast, not fictional facts.
W05 Dickens: contrasting consequences, not ornate long antitheses or false balance.
W06 Munroe: a precise question and stepwise mechanism supported by illustrations, not free invention in factual reporting.
